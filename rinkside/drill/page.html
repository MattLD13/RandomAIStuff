    How to play
    
    
      
      
    1. One real game a day. A big one: a Stanley Cup Final, a playoff series, or a rivalry night. Your team is down a goal late in the third, and in real life they lost.
      2. 
      
    2. Call every shift. Faceoff plays, zone entries, cycle or seam pass, forecheck or sit back. At a stoppage, or when you have the puck, you can pull the goalie. You get one timeout: it rests your top unit.
      3. 
      
    3. Every call is graded. The game replays the same spot about 3,000 times with each call you could have made. A is the best call available; F gave up more than 6 points of win chance (edges count for more when your chances are slim). Calls made after the game is out of reach are not graded.
      4. 
      
    4. Tie it and keep going. Regular season: 3-on-3 overtime, then a shootout with your real shooters and their real career numbers against the real goalie. Playoffs: 20-minute sudden-death periods until someone scores.
      5. 
      
    5. Then the reveal. See what the real coach did and how the game really ended.
      6. 
    
    
    
    Starting spots, rosters and shootout numbers are real (NHL play-by-play, 2010–2026). The model's scoring rates are tuned so that its typical coach matches about 950 real endings like these: real teams down one this late tied it 19.6% of the time and won 10.1%. Play-by-play lines inside your game are simulated.
