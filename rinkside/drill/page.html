    How to play
    
    
      
      
    1. A real game, every day. A Stanley Cup Final, a playoff series or a rivalry night. Your team is down a goal late in the third, and in real life they lost. You have their real lines.
      2. 
      
    2. One call per possession. With the puck: go now, set it up, get it to the point, crash the net, or dump and change when your legs are gone. In their end: shoot now or work it low. Off a draw in their end: the one-timer set play. Tap a call and the possession plays out.
      3. 
      
    3. The goalie and the timeout are calls too. "Pull the goalie, then attack" puts six on the ice. "Five forwards, all in" is the big gamble. "Timeout, draw up a play" rests your bench and runs a set play off the draw.
      4. 
      
    4. Without the puck: play it straight (they burn clock), forecheck hard (turnover or an odd-man rush the other way), or sell out late.
      5. 
      
    5. Who gets it. Leave it on Anyone, or feed one of your players: his real shooting this season counts. Feeding a star on the bench sends his line out.
      6. 
      
    6. Read what they are showing. A collapsing box gives you the point. Wingers jumping your points leave the slot. A team sagging off leaks rebounds. A team changing can be caught with speed. An icing traps their tired line on the ice.
      7. 
      
    7. Every call is graded against the other calls you had, each replayed about 800 times from the same spot. Your score (0 to 100) compares you with a typical NHL bench; 50 is typical.
      8. 
      
    8. Tie it and keep going. Regular season: 3-on-3 overtime, then a shootout with your real shooters. Playoffs: sudden-death overtime.
      9. 
    
    
    
    Starting spots, lineups and shootout numbers are real (NHL play-by-play, 2010–2026). Lines, D pairs, who was on the ice and each center's faceoff record come from that game's NHL shift charts and play-by-play. The model is tuned so its typical bench matches about 950 real endings like these: down one this late, real teams tied it 19.6% of the time and won 10.1%. The opponent's looks, the plays inside each shift and each goalie's tendencies are simulated.
