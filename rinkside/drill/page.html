    How to play
    
    
      
      
    1. A real game, every day. A Stanley Cup Final, a playoff series or a rivalry night. Your team is down a goal late in the third, and in real life they lost.
      2. 
      
    2. Read the look. Before every call the other team shows you something: their center owns the dot, a 1-3-1 trap, a sagging box, an open back door. Each look makes some calls better and others worse.
      3. 
      
    3. Make the call on the ice. Every call is a target on the rink: tap the net to shoot, a teammate to pass, the corner to dump it in, their puck carrier to go after him. Or drag the puck to a target, tap a call below, or press 1 to 4. At a stoppage, or when you have the puck, you can pull the goalie (P). One timeout per game (T).
      4. 
      
    4. Watch it play out. Every call is checked against every other call you could have made, each replayed 3,000 times from the same spot. Green means you read it right.
      5. 
      
    5. Your score. 0 to 100: how your calls compare with a typical NHL bench from the same spots. 50 is a typical bench.
      6. 
      
    6. Tie it and keep going. Regular season: 3-on-3 overtime, then a shootout with your real shooters. Playoffs: sudden-death overtime until someone scores. In overtime each call covers a longer stretch of play.
      7. 
    
    
    
    Starting spots, lineups and shootout numbers are real (NHL play-by-play, 2010–2026). The model is tuned so its typical bench matches about 950 real endings like these: down one this late, real teams tied it 19.6% of the time and won 10.1%. The looks, the play-by-play inside your game and each goalie's tendencies are simulated.
